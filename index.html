Phone Vitals Lab

Measure your pulse with the camera, and your breathing with the motion sensors and a sonar made from the speaker and microphone. Every result can be checked against a reference and saved to a validation log.

  3. If the signal stays weak, roll your finger slightly toward the flash or press more lightly.
  4. Rest your hand on a table and keep still for 30 seconds.

    Frequency spectrum

    How strongly each rhythm between 42 and 210 BPM is present in the pulse wave. The axis is in BPM and the tallest peak is the heart rate. A smaller bump at double that rate is the pulse's harmonic.

    How the page reads your pulse

    1. Average each frame. Every camera frame is shrunk to 40 × 40 pixels and its red, green and blue values are averaged. With light shining through your fingertip, red dominates.
    2. Check for a finger. Frames only count while they are strongly red. Lifting your finger restarts the 30 seconds.
    3. Drop the warm-up. The first 2 seconds after your finger lands are discarded while the camera adjusts its exposure.
    4. Even out the timing. Frames arrive at slightly uneven intervals, so the signal is resampled to exactly 30 samples per second.
    5. Keep heart-rate frequencies only. A band-pass filter keeps 0.7 to 3.5 Hz (42 to 210 BPM). It removes slow drift from breathing and hand movement and fast sensor noise.
    6. Find the rhythm two ways. The frequency spectrum gives the dominant rate. Separately, each beat's peak is located, and the gaps between beats give a second rate and the HRV figure.
    7. Grade the signal. Quality compares the power at the heart rate and its harmonic with everything else in the band, and checks that the two rates agree within 8%.

    Limits of this version

    • This is a learning tool, not a medical device.
    • The camera's automatic exposure keeps adjusting, which adds slow drift. The filter removes most of it.
    • Treat HRV as experimental. A 30 fps camera is coarse for beat-to-beat timing.
    • Movement and pressing too hard cause most bad readings.
    • Switching apps or locking the screen stops the measurement.
    Paste into a spreadsheet or Python to try your own filters.

    Measure breathing

    Phone on chest

    1. Turn the media volume up. The sonar plays a tone too high for most adults to hear.
    2. Lie on your back. Place the phone flat on your breastbone, screen up, with the bottom edge (speaker and microphone) pointing toward your chin.
    3. Rest your arms by your sides and breathe normally. A chime marks the start and the end of the 60-second window.
    4. To check accuracy, count each breath out between the two chimes, or ask someone to watch and count.
    1. Turn the media volume up.
    2. Sit back or lie down. Prop the phone 20 to 40 cm from your chest, bottom edge (speaker and microphone) pointing at your chest, for example on a pillow on your lap.
    3. Don't hold it. Hand movement is larger than breathing movement and swamps the sonar.
    4. Breathe normally. Count each breath out between the start and end chimes if you are checking accuracy.

    Children and pets may hear the sonar tone as a faint whine. Try a higher tone if it bothers anyone.

      How the page measures breathing

      1. Motion sensors. As your chest rises, the phone tilts by a fraction of a degree. The accelerometer sees gravity shift slightly between its axes, and the gyroscope sees the rotation. The page combines the axes into the one direction that moves the most.
      2. Sonar. The speaker plays a steady tone. The microphone hears it back, mixed with echoes. When your chest moves a few millimetres toward or away from the phone, the echo's timing shifts, which rotates the tone's phase. The page tracks that phase.
      3. Find the rhythm. Each signal is filtered to 6 to 42 breaths/min. Breaths are counted, and the frequency spectrum gives a second estimate.
      4. Grade each method. A method counts as clear when its signal repeats regularly from one breath to the next and its two estimates agree.
      5. Cross-check. If both methods are clear and within 2 breaths/min of each other, the result blends them, giving more weight to the cleaner signal. If they disagree, the cleaner one wins and the page says so.

      Limits of this version

      • This is a learning tool, not a medical device.
      • Talking, coughing and shifting position break the rhythm for a few breaths.
      • Phone speakers and microphones vary in how well they handle ultrasound. The live view shows how strongly the tone reaches the microphone.
      • Paying attention to your breathing tends to slow it. That is fine for testing, because the page and your count see the same breaths.
